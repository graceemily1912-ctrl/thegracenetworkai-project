# The Grace Network Brand Contract

## Named centre

The Grace Network sells one thing: a private operator’s day with Emily Grace.

The page should feel like a discreet invitation from someone whose time is scarce,
not a curriculum catalogue, technology consultancy, or online course funnel.

## Audience

Established founders and operators with real revenue, products, responsibilities,
or capital already in motion. They do not need technology explained to them. They
need judgment, pattern recognition, protected time, and a room calibrated to their
level.

## Voice

- Direct, warm, composed, and specific.
- Short sentences followed by occasional spacious editorial passages.
- Confidence comes from named operating proof, not superlatives.
- Emily speaks as a capable human with a full life.
- Price is stated plainly and never defended with inflated ROI arithmetic.

## Forbidden public-facing language

- AI, artificial intelligence, LLM, ChatGPT, Claude
- agent, agentic, automation, automate
- prompt, prompt pack, template pack
- accelerator, transformation, implementation sprint
- webinar, cohort, course library, curriculum catalogue
- autonomous, optimized workflows, cutting-edge
- “six-figure day” or other implied outcome claims
- tourists, freebie hunters, bargain-bin, hobbyists

Internal field names and backend status values may remain where changing them
would break stored data, but none may appear in public copy.

## Required proof

- Multiple six-figure businesses operated solo.
- AUVIE — district education.
- KOLA Academy — nature-based learning.
- Medvo — health.
- FamilyTerms — family.
- The Kola Fund — charity.
- Trading systems — live capital, historical examples only.
- Solo parenting and a full real life.

## Visual contract

- Editorial hospitality, not SaaS: Baskerville/Iowan-style display type with a
  clean humanist sans serif.
- Okanagan landscape as atmosphere, never stock-photo decoration.
- Deep ink, parchment, warm brass, and restrained oxblood accents.
- Strong asymmetry, hairline rules, numbered details, and generous negative space.
- No floating gradient blobs, generic rounded card grids, pill overload, glowing
  buttons, dashboard chrome, or icon collections.
- Motion is restrained to the first paint and hover details; native scrolling wins.

## Information arc

1. The promise: run more without life running you.
2. The proof: companies, charity, trading, parenting.
3. The day: bring the live operating problem.
4. The price: $15,000, stated without theatre.
5. The filter: clear fit and non-fit.
6. The place: O’Rourke Winery.
7. The invitation: a mutual fit call.

## Failure modes

- Explaining the method until the page becomes a wall of text.
- Sounding like a technology consultant or online educator.
- Repeating exclusivity instead of demonstrating it.
- Using luxury clichés without real proof.
- Making financial performance sound promised or replicable.
- Hiding the price or creating false urgency.
- Letting legacy application pages look like another brand.

## Ship checklist

- [ ] Forbidden public language is absent from all public routes.
- [ ] Every required proof point appears accurately.
- [ ] The homepage has one dominant promise and one dominant action.
- [ ] Desktop and mobile preserve editorial hierarchy.
- [ ] Keyboard focus and reduced-motion states remain usable.
- [ ] Booking, application, payment, and status paths still function.
- [ ] Build passes and the exact commit is verified live.

## Review posture

Emily is the brand authority. Conservative legal wording ships with the page;
formal legal review is after-the-fact and does not block ordinary copy changes.
